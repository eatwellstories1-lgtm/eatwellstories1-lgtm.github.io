About the blog

About EatWellStories

EatWellStories is a healthy-eating niche blog focused on one clear promise: simple, balanced meals and snacks that follow mainstream nutrition guidance. We publish list-style food guides, ingredient deep-dives, prep-ahead lunch and dinner recipes, and beginner diet guides such as the Mediterranean diet plan.

Who writes EatWellStories?

The blog is written and edited by Youssef EL MONTASSIR as part of an International E-Marketing semester project. Every article carries an author byline, a published date, a reading time, a sources list and a medical disclaimer so readers can judge the evidence behind each recommendation.

Who is the audience?

Readers in the United Kingdom and United States who want trustworthy, easy-to-follow nutrition guidance without jargon: busy parents preparing family dinners, office workers packing lunch, anyone building a snack routine, and readers beginning a balanced-eating pattern such as the Mediterranean diet.

Editorial and E-E-A-T standards

  • Every guide cites authoritative sources: the World Health Organization, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, the NHS Eatwell Guide, USDA FoodData Central, Cleveland Clinic and published peer-reviewed trials.
  • Articles explain what evidence shows and what it does not prove; foods are presented as part of an eating pattern, not cures.
  • Numbers come from published nutrient databases; recipes use everyday ingredients and practical timings.
  • The site maintains a medical disclaimer: content is general information, not individual medical advice.

How the site is structured

The pillar guide, 50 super healthy foods, links outward to every supporting topic: ingredients (eggs, tomatoes), breakfast foods, easy dinner ideas, healthy snack ideas, packed work lunches and the Mediterranean diet for beginners. Category pages group the cluster, and related-post modules keep readers moving through the content.

Monetization and transparency

Once consistent traffic develops, the blog may use Amazon Associates affiliate links on cookbooks, kitchen tools, tinned fish and olive oil; display advertising placed below the fold; a free lead-magnet meal planner; and clearly labelled sponsored posts. Affiliate relationships are disclosed where they appear.
